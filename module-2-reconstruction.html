Module 2 · 1865–1877

Reconstruction and the Freedmen’s Bureau

What did lived freedom require after slavery?

Image source: U.S. National Park Service, Reconstruction-era African American education. Add full credit and rights information in SOURCES.md.

Source B: Reconstruction and the Freedmen’s Bureau

After the Civil War, formerly enslaved people had to build new lives in a society shaped by slavery. The Freedmen’s Bureau was created to assist this transition. Its records include letters, reports, labour contracts, school records, certificates, censuses and legal statements called affidavits.

These records show that freedom involved more than the end of legal slavery. Formerly enslaved people tried to reunite families, negotiate wages, establish schools, protect themselves in courts and claim the rights of citizens. Employers, landowners and local authorities often resisted these demands.

The records show both change and continuity: slavery had been legally abolished, but unequal power, racism, poverty and violence continued.
