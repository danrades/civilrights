Module 3 · 1896

Plessy v. Ferguson and segregation

Why was “separate but equal” not equal in practice?

Image source: Add the exact archive, creator, date and rights statement in SOURCES.md. Use a public-domain or clearly licensed historical image.

Source C: Plessy v. Ferguson

A Louisiana law required railway companies to provide “equal but separate accommodations for the white, and colored races.”

The Supreme Court upheld the law. The majority argued that separation did not necessarily mean that one race was inferior to another. This became known as “separate but equal.”

Justice John Marshall Harlan disagreed. He argued that laws separating citizens by race could place Black citizens in a condition of legal inferiority and weaken the meaning of freedom.

The decision helped support legal segregation for many decades.
